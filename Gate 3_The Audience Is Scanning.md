# **Gate 3 — The Audience Is Scanning**

## **🧱 Foundational Truth**

**People scan to make sense, fast.**

## **🎯 Objective**

In a choice-rich environment, audiences have only a fraction of a second to begin making sense of what they see.

They don't process every detail. They scan for enough meaningful information to quickly understand what they're looking at.

The objective is not to communicate everything at once.

**It is to communicate enough, fast enough.**

## **🤔 Why It Matters**

In streaming services and social feeds, audiences are surrounded by choice and control the pace at which they move through it.

Every scroll, swipe, or row presents another option. Moving on requires almost no effort, so audiences have little reason to spend time deciphering an image they don't immediately understand.

People can begin making sense of an image within a fraction of a second.

Information that takes too long to understand may never get the opportunity to communicate. Something can be present in an image and still go unseen if the audience has already moved on.

**An image therefore needs to work at the speed of the audience.**

The faster audiences can make sense of what they see, the greater the opportunity for the image to communicate what matters.

## **👤 How the Audience Thinks**

Audiences don't process every part of an image before making sense of it.

When scanning quickly, they use the most immediately available visual signals to begin understanding what they see. They don't need to understand everything in the first moment. They need enough **Immediate Meaning** to make sense of the image quickly.

**Immediate Meaning is the information an audience can understand at a glance, before looking more closely.**

What counts as meaningful depends on the image and the context in which it appears. More information does not necessarily create more understanding. If the audience has to work to figure out what they're looking at, the image is asking for time they may never give it.

## **🧐 Gate Evaluation**

An image passes Gate 3 when enough Immediate Meaning is available for the audience to make sense of it at a glance.

### **What is the Immediate Meaning of the image?**

Consider what an audience can understand before looking more closely.

Immediate Meaning might communicate:

* **Who or what** the image is about  
* **What** is happening  
* **Where** it is taking place  
* **How it feels**

An image does not need to communicate all of these. The question is whether enough meaningful information is immediately available for the audience to make sense of what they see.

### **What only becomes clear when you look longer?**

Images can contain layers of meaning that reward closer attention.

The problem arises when information essential to making sense of the image depends on details the audience may never reach.

Remember: **Gate 3 evaluates how quickly meaning becomes available, not how much meaning the image contains.**

## **🧠 Reviewer's Creed**

**The audience doesn't need to understand everything.**

**They need enough to make sense of the image, fast.**

**Details should reward attention, not require it.**
